How to Prevent Common Football Injuries Pre-Season

Choosing the right cricket bat is an art form. The process begins deep in the forests of England, where Salix alba coerulea (the specific species of white willow) is grown specifically for cricket bat production.

Understanding the Cleft

When a tree is felled, it is split into wedges called clefts. These clefts are graded based on grain structure, blemishes, and weight. A Grade 1 cleft will have straight, even grains (usually 6-10) and no prominent blemishes on the playing face.

"The bat is an extension of the batsman. The sweet spot isn't just about thickness, it's about the balance and pick-up."

The Pressing Process

Before a bat can be shaped, the willow must be pressed. This compresses the fibers, making the wood harder and more resilient to the impact of a cricket ball. Over-pressing makes the bat dead, while under-pressing risks severe damage during play.
